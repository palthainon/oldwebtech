## Behavior

- When the pharmacy workflow assigns a technician to identify or route an issue that requires pharmacist intervention, follow that workflow and the applicable state technician scope requirements. PTCE examples include drug utilization review, adverse drug events, OTC recommendations, therapeutic substitutions, misuse, adherence, post-immunization delivery care, allergies, and drug interactions.
- The PTCB examples identify the intervention topic. They do not assign a universal technician action or define the pharmacist's clinical decision.

## Defaults or baseline

- The PTCE Knowledge Reference identifies the listed concerns as issues requiring pharmacist intervention. It classifies state requirements for pharmacy-technician roles and responsibilities as recommended knowledge.

## Gotchas

- Do not infer who identifies, routes, or resolves an intervention issue from the PTCE examples.
- The PTCE examples do not replace the pharmacy's escalation procedure or applicable state scope requirements.

## Verify

- Have a qualified pharmacist or pharmacy technician confirm the current pharmacy escalation workflow, the applicable state scope, and the technician role before operational use or question development.

## Applies to

- PTCE 2026 Patient Safety and Quality Assurance, objective 3.3.
