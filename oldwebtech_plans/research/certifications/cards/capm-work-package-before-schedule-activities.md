## Behavior

- When planning a predictive project, describe the deliverables and lower-level work packages in a work breakdown structure (WBS) before turning those packages into the activities, dependencies, and durations of the project schedule. If a proposed WBS element describes an action or timing rather than an output or scope component, place that detail in schedule planning.

## Defaults or baseline

- A WBS is a deliverable-oriented hierarchy of project scope. Its lowest-level components are work packages, which provide the basis for elaborating schedule activities.

## Gotchas

- Treating a list of timed tasks as the WBS can obscure a missing deliverable: the schedule may look complete while the agreed scope is not represented.

## Verify

- Compare the project's proposed WBS elements with its scope and deliverables. For one work package, identify the output it represents, then identify the separate activities needed to produce it. This card is based on PMI publications; no live project plan was reviewed.
