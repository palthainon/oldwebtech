## Behavior

- When a pharmacy workflow applies a control for high-alert/risk or look-alike/sound-alike medications, use the control as that workflow and applicable state technician scope require. PTCE examples include separating inventory, Tall Man lettering, bar code use, and limiting error-prone abbreviations.
- Use the pharmacy's applicable process to match a prescription or medication order to the correct patient.

## Defaults or baseline

- PTCB lists these as examples of error-prevention strategies. The outline does not prescribe a universal control sequence, technician role, or final-check role.

## Gotchas

- Do not treat a listed control as a universal technician duty or assume that one control replaces the pharmacy's required checks.
- The outline gives examples of error-prevention strategies. The pharmacy procedure and applicable state scope determine which controls apply at a processing step.

## Verify

- Have a qualified pharmacist or pharmacy technician confirm the current workflow, the applicable state scope, the controls used at the processing step, and the technician role before operational use or question development.

## Applies to

- PTCE 2026 Patient Safety and Quality Assurance, objectives 3.1 and 3.2.
