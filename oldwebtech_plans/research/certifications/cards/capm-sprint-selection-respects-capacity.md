## Behavior

- In a Scrum iteration, use the Product Owner's ordered backlog to discuss the most important work, then let the Developers select a feasible set of items for the Sprint after considering their recent performance, upcoming capacity, and Definition of Done. The Scrum Team defines a Sprint Goal; the selected items and delivery plan form the Sprint Backlog.

## Defaults or baseline

- Product Backlog order expresses the Product Owner's priority. Selection for a particular Sprint is a collaborative planning decision informed by what the Developers can complete; backlog order alone is not a promise to finish every requested item in that Sprint.

## Gotchas

- Treating the whole priority list as a committed Sprint plan ignores capacity. If a selected set proves too large while planning, revisit the selection with the Product Owner instead of counting unfinished items as a credible forecast. This is Scrum-specific planning guidance, not a universal rule for every adaptive framework.

## Verify

- For a sample Sprint plan, identify the Product Goal and ordered Product Backlog, the Sprint Goal, the items selected by Developers, and the capacity evidence used in their forecast. Compare those artifacts with the Scrum Guide's Product Owner, Sprint Planning, and Sprint Backlog sections. No live project plan was reviewed.
