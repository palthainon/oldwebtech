## Behavior

- When a project is subject to a regulatory, security, safety, or sustainability obligation, confirm which requirements apply with the responsible subject-matter or compliance functions. Record how the project will address and measure each applicable obligation, including work and reviews that cross team or project boundaries.

## Defaults or baseline

- Treat compliance as a project concern that can affect multiple processes, not only as a customer requirement or a single scope item. Use the organization's applicable rules and procedures as inputs, and identify the people responsible for interpreting and checking them.

## Gotchas

- Assuming that a client requirement or a late acceptance audit captures every applicable rule can leave technical, legal, organizational, or control obligations undiscovered until testing, causing rework. A project manager coordinates the people and activities needed for compliance; the cited PMI paper does not assign the project manager sole technical or legal authority to determine conformity.

## Verify

- For a scenario with a newly identified compliance obligation, trace it from confirmation and classification through threats, planned actions, and a way to measure compliance. Check that an appropriate specialist or control function participates and that unresolved noncompliance has an owner and documented follow-up. These are review prompts drawn from PMI guidance; this card does not define jurisdiction-specific compliance criteria.

## Applies to

- PMP Business Environment Task 2, Plan and manage project compliance. No question IDs assigned.
